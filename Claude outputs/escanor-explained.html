Inside Escanor

How the algorithm turns 13 pages of NFL.com team stats into a weekly pick against the spread, traced with the real Week 2, 2026 numbers. Part 2 covers where it goes wrong and what would make it more accurate.

Part 1

The thought process

The pipeline, per game

Escanor asks one question for every matchup: whose offense has the bigger edge over the opposing defense? It scores both teams the same way and picks the higher total.

1

Load season-to-date team stats

Offense and defense stats for all 32 teams, pulled from NFL.com (109 columns). These are season totals and averages; nothing about injuries, weather or the betting line is used here.

2

Match each offense against the other team's defense

For every stat, divide what the offense does by what the opposing defense allows. Done twice: away offense vs home defense, and home offense vs away defense.

ratio = offense stat ÷ opposing defense's allowed stat

Downs is the exception: offense conversion % ÷ (100 − defense's allowed conversion %), i.e. against the defense's stop rate.

3

Blend ratios into four category scores

Each category is a weighted sum of its ratios. A team that exactly matches the defense on everything scores about 100 per category.

Passing: Cmp% 25 · Rate 35 · 1st% 10 · Sacks 15 · Yds/Att 20 Rushing: YPC 40 · TDs 15 · 1st% 20 · Yards 25 Receiving: Yds/Rec 50 · TDs 15 · 1st% 35 Downs: 3rd 80 · 4th 20
4

Weight the categories into one team total

total = 0.33·Passing + 0.22·Rushing + 0.18·Receiving + 0.27·Downs
5

Pick the higher total; the gap is the Confidence Factor

pick = max(away_total, home_total) CF = |away_total − home_total|

This is a straight-up winner pick. The spread is attached afterward as a label: if the pick is the favorite it shows −4.5, otherwise +4.5.

6

Rank the week

Games are sorted by CF. The top 5 become Top 5; every game at or above the season's rolling median CF (19.39 this week) becomes Above Median.

What Escanor actually cares about

Multiply each stat's sub-weight by its category weight and you get its real share of a team's total. One stat, 3rd-down conversion, carries almost as much weight as all of rushing combined (21.6 vs 22.0).

Passing 34.7Rushing 22.0Receiving 18.0Downs 27.0

Passing sub-weights add to 105, not 100, so passing is really 34.7% of the total, not 33%. Sacks are marked red: see Part 2.

Where the "confidence" can come from nothing

Raiders @ Chargers is this week's lowest pick: Raiders +6.5 at CF 0.97. Most of Las Vegas's edge comes from one division by zero.

Receiving TDs: 3 ÷ 0

The Chargers defense has allowed 0 receiving TDs, so the ratio can't be computed. Escanor falls back to the raw count, 3.0, three times what a neutral ratio of 1.0 would give. At weight 15 × 0.18, that is worth +5.4 points to the Raiders. Without it, the Raiders total drops below the Chargers' and the pick flips to Chargers −6.5.

Part 2

Making it more accurate

Ranked by how much each is likely to change the picks. None of these are applied yet. Anything that changes the math should be backtested against 2025-26 before it replaces the current formula.

High

Passing, rushing and receiving reward facing good defenses

Offense ÷ defense-allowed gets smaller as the defense gets worse. In the example, the Panthers' 107.5 passer rating vs a Falcons defense allowing 81.4 scores 1.32. Against a defense allowing 124.1 it would score 0.87, so an easier opponent makes Carolina look weaker. Downs already does it the right way, against the defense's stop rate.

Fix: compare both sides to the league average, e.g. (offense ÷ league avg) × (defense allowed ÷ league avg). For stats where lower is better for the offense, flip the ratio.

High

The spread never affects the pick

Escanor picks who wins, then labels it with the line. A CF of 14.7 on Ravens −8.5 says nothing about whether Baltimore wins by 9 or more. Picking against the spread only works if the model estimates a margin and compares it to the line.

Fix: fit CF against last season's actual point margins (a simple linear fit is enough to start), convert CF to projected points, and pick the side where projected margin − spread is largest. Rank by that edge instead of raw CF.

High

Sacks count in the wrong direction

Offensive_Passing_Sck is sacks the offense gave up. More of them raises the score. The Falcons got a 2.00 ratio (the highest number in their column) purely from a leaky line.

Fix: invert it (defense sacks made ÷ offense sacks allowed), and use sack rate per dropback rather than counts.

Medium

Divide-by-zero fallbacks inflate small numbers

TD and sack ratios fall back to the raw count when the denominator is 0, which created the Raiders pick above.

Fix: add 1 to both sides before dividing ((off + 1) ÷ (def + 1)), or cap every ratio at something like 0.5 to 2.0.

Medium

Early-season stats are one or two games deep

Week 2 runs on a single game per team. Ratios like 2.16 for rush yards are mostly noise.

Fix: blend with last season's final stats, fading the weight: roughly 70% last season in Week 2, 50% by Week 4, 0% by Week 8.

Medium

Season totals ignore bye weeks

TDs, sacks and rush yards are totals. From Week 5 on, a team that has played one game fewer looks worse on all of them.

Fix: divide totals by games played before computing ratios.

Low

No home-field or turnover signal

Home field is worth roughly 1.5 points and isn't modeled. The scraper also pulls fumbles, interceptions and scoring, but Escanor never uses them. Turnover margin is one of the stronger single predictors of point margin.

Fix: once CF is in points, add a home-field constant. Test adding turnovers as a fifth category.

Low

The weights are hand-picked

33/22/18/27 and every sub-weight are judgment calls, never fit to results.

Fix: after the fixes above, fit the weights on past seasons with a regression (predict margin from the category scores), then check them on a held-out season.

Suggested order

  1. Build a backtest first. Collect per-game stats and final scores for 2025-26 and score the current formula against the closing spread. Without this, every change is a guess.
  2. Apply the bug-level fixes (sack direction, zero fallbacks, per-game rates) and re-run the backtest.
  3. Fix the ratio direction with league-average normalization, then backtest again.
  4. Convert CF to points and pick against the spread by edge.
  5. Add early-season blending, home field and turnovers, keeping each only if the backtest improves.

A useful target: breaking even at standard −110 odds takes a 52.4% win rate. Last season's full slate finished at 45.1%.
